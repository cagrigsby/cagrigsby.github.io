Like a lot of other, smarter people I look at the modern Internet and feel disappointed with the state of things. It feels like a fractured, angry place much of the time, and moreover, it feels stupid. I spend too much of my time on Reddit and Hacker News, and I find myself being slowly boiled by the descent of quality in conversation. I don't want to be the person claiming without evidence that things used to be better, but there's something there. Every once in a while, I'll find myself reading the comments in a decades-old post and note how differently users communicated, in particular how much more closely they adhered to a standard of communication. 

## A Silly Example

I was googling a video I remembered from my youth, and I saw [this post on reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/movies/comments/d9kn3/there_will_be_bud_an_amazing_and_hilarious_parody/) discussing it. There are only 17 comments, and as expected, some of them don't offer much. One of them is from [/u/spam-police](https://www.reddit.com/user/spam_police/) (who evidently found better things to do 8 years ago) complaining about misattribution. Another user points out that "lol" adds nothing to the discussion. This kind of self-policing of the community is completely gone. Spam is not called out, "reposts" are expected, and users no longer explain themselves if they edit a comment after being replied to which often makes the responder look like an asshole. 

A question for you, beloved reader: Do you know what upvotes are for? Do you know their purpose? From [the help page](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/7419626610708-What-are-upvotes-and-downvotes), "Upvotes show that redditors think content is positively contributing to a community or the site as a whole...If you like something, be it a post or a comment, and **you think it contributes to a conversation**, upvote it!" They are not "likes," they are explicitly meant to denote that a post or comment contributes something to a discussion.
